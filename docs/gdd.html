Game Title & Genre

Working title, genre framing, and the alternates considered along the way.

Genre: Survival & Resource-Management, played as an arcade score-attack — single-session runs of a few minutes each, built for mobile portrait, one-thumb play.

Salvage Storm

Leans into the repair-race framing over the diving itself — reads more like a management sim than an arcade sprint.

Undertow

Moodier, single-word, focuses on the threat and the current rather than the run-and-collect loop.

Riptide Run

Closest in sound to the endless-runner comps (Subway Surfers, Temple Run) — strong genre signal, less specific to diving.

Recommendation

Keep Depth Rush. It reads instantly as fast + underwater, works as a two-word app icon label, and doesn't overpromise story depth the prototype isn't building.

Target Player & Pitch

Who this is for, and the one paragraph that sells it.

  • Audience Subway Surfers / Temple Run / Alto's Odyssey players
  • Also appeals to Resource-tension fans (Don't Starve-lite)
  • Session 90 sec – 5 min, one run
  • Controls One thumb, portrait only
  • Age 10+, no reading-heavy onboarding
  • Context Commute, queue, break — phone in one hand

"Depth Rush is a one-thumb underwater survival sprint. Your ship is wrecked, its parts scattered across the seabed, and a storm is five minutes from making landfall. Dive, scavenge gear and salvage, dodge sharks, and race the clock back to the ship — every second underwater is a bet against your oxygen. The seabed reshuffles every run, so the only way to beat your last score is to dive again."

03/ 07

How to Play

Three touch inputs, four HUD readouts. Nothing that needs a tutorial longer than one dive.

TAP

Swim toward the tapped point. Tapping while touching a floating part, pickup, or the ship auto-triggers collect / return — no separate button.

HOLD

On a drillable rock or sea mount, drills the surface open. A fill-ring tracks progress at the touch point; releasing early cancels. O2 drains at 2× while drilling.

BOOST
(UI button)

Bottom-right thumb zone. Burns a burst of fin speed to break away from a shark. O2 drains at 3× while active, with a short cooldown after release.

O2 GAUGE

Top-left vertical bar. Drains continuously; shifts green → amber under 40% → pulsing red under 15%.

STORM TIMER

Top-right, counts down from 5:00. Inside the last 60 seconds it pulses and the water visibly darkens.

SONAR PING

Bottom-left dial. Sweeps faster and brighter the closer the diver is to an undiscovered part, with a bearing tick toward it.

04/ 07

Core Loop

One dive, start to finish — the loop that has to feel good before anything else gets built.

0M

Surface

Ship deploys the diver. Storm timer starts at 5:00, O2 tank fills to 100%.

−5 to −15M

Descend

Tap-swim toward sonar bearings; visibility narrows as the seabed approaches.

−15M

Seabed

Reach rock and sea-mount clusters; hold to drill them open. O2 drains faster the whole time.

−15M

Scavenge

Grab floodlights (light up dark pockets), spare O2 tanks (refill the gauge), fins (permanent speed boost for the run), and the ship parts sonar is guiding you toward.

−15M

Evade

Sharks patrol fixed lanes. Enter a danger radius and a warning pulse starts; hold BOOST to break away at the cost of O2.

−8M

Ascend

Carrying at least one part, tap-swim back toward the surface and the ship — more parts in hand, higher the payoff, higher the risk of staying down.

0M

Auto-Repair & Score

Reaching the ship with parts in hand triggers repair automatically. Parts, O2 remaining, time remaining, and risk bonus tally into a score.

↻ RUN ENDS → SEABED RESHUFFLES → DIVE AGAIN
Fail State

O2 hits zero, a shark catches you, or the storm timer hits 0:00 — any of the three ends the run immediately at the same unified Game Over screen, showing the score breakdown, then Retry.

What's in This Prototype

10 days, one build target: prove the full loop feels good — not ship a finished game. Every system below is in, at simplified fidelity: one seabed, minimal animation, placeholder-grade art and audio throughout.

In the Prototype

  • One seabed scene (rocky reef + shipwreck silhouette), Three.js, portrait camera
  • Tap-to-swim diver movement — simple procedural bob/tilt, no full rig
  • O2 system with live gauge; baseline, drill, and boost drain multipliers
  • 5-minute storm timer with late-game visual pressure (darkening, pulsing HUD)
  • Per-run randomized placement of parts, drillable rocks, floodlights, spare O2, fins, sea mounts, shark lanes
  • Sonar ping (directional bearing hint, not a full radar map)
  • Drilling interaction — hold, fill-ring, releases a part or O2 tank
  • Fin pickups (permanent per-run speed multiplier)
  • 1–2 sharks with patrol + chase-if-close steering behavior
  • Boost button — speed burst, higher O2 drain, cooldown
  • Auto-repair on return to ship
  • Unified Game Over for all three fail states
  • Score formula, local high score, Retry loop

Later — See Future Vision

  • Multiple biomes / levels
  • Meta-progression, currency, permanent unlocks
  • Daily seeds, global leaderboards, backend services
  • Cosmetic customization
  • Story beats, cutscenes, additional characters
  • Full sound design (prototype ships with placeholder SFX only)

Progression & Signature Twist

Nothing carries over between runs by design — the pressure resets every dive. That's deliberate.

Progression here happens within a single dive, not across runs: a floodlight makes the next dark pocket readable, a pair of fins makes every subsequent swim cheaper in O2, a spare tank buys you the time to risk one more drill. Each pickup compounds the ones before it for the rest of that run — then it's gone, and the next dive starts from zero again.

1. A Fixed-Length Tension Arc

Unlike an endless runner's infinite ramp, every run is the same five minutes — so the skill ceiling isn't survival, it's efficiency. Past the 60-second mark the storm visibly closes in: visibility drops, the water desaturates, the HUD pulses. Same clock, escalating pressure, every single run.

2. Risk-Adjacent Scoring

Score rewards proximity to danger, not just avoidance of it — each close call with a shark (entering its danger radius and escaping alive) banks a risk bonus. Playing safe is viable; playing bold scores higher. That's the pull for one more dive, the same way a near-miss in Subway Surfers feels better than a clean lane change.

Score Formula
SCORE = (PARTS × 200) + (O2 REMAINING × 5) + (TIME REMAINING × 3) + (CLOSE CALLS × 50)

Future State Vision

Where this goes once the prototype proves the loop — grouped by what each addition is actually for.

Content

  • Additional biomes: kelp forest, shipwreck graveyard, deep trench
  • New hazards per biome: currents, electric eels, jellyfish swarms
  • More shark species and pack behavior

Progression

  • Meta-currency ("Salvage Points") earned per run
  • Permanent upgrades: starting O2 capacity, fin speed, drill speed, sonar range

Replayability & Social

  • Daily seeded challenge runs
  • Global / friend leaderboards
  • Ghost replays of best runs

Narrative

  • Light story layer: other stranded divers to find and rescue
  • Radio-chatter beats between runs

Storm Escalation

  • Lightning strikes that briefly blind or scramble the sonar
  • Stronger current pushing the diver off course late in a run

Accessibility & Monetization

  • Colorblind-safe HUD states, adjustable control sensitivity
  • Rewarded-ad continues; cosmetic-only IAP — no pay-to-win
